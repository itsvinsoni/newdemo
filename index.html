Speed, Velocity & Acceleration

Average Speed vs Average Velocity

Average Speed: Total distance covered divided by the total time taken. It's a scalar quantity and always positive.

Avg Speed = Total Distance / Total Time

Average Velocity: Total displacement divided by the total time taken. It's a vector quantity and can be positive, negative, or zero.

Avg Velocity = Total Displacement / Total Time

Instantaneous Velocity & Speed

  • Inst. Velocity (v): Velocity at a specific instant.
  • v = dx/dt
  • It's the slope of the x-t graph at that instant.
  • Inst. Speed: Magnitude of instantaneous velocity.
  • Speed = |v|
  • Speedometer reading is instantaneous speed.

Acceleration (a)

  • Rate of change of velocity.
  • Avg. Acceleration: Change in velocity / Time interval.
  • aavg = Δv/Δt
  • Inst. Acceleration: Acceleration at a specific instant.
  • a = dv/dt = d²x/dt²
  • Vector quantity (m/s²).
  • Positive (speeding up), Negative (slowing down - retardation), Zero (constant velocity).

Uniform & Non-Uniform Motion

Uniform Motion

Object covers equal distances in equal time intervals.

Velocity is constant.

Acceleration is zero.

Non-Uniform Motion

Object covers unequal distances in equal time intervals.

Velocity is variable.

Acceleration is non-zero.

Equations of Uniformly Accelerated Motion

Conditions & Variables

Condition for Use:

These equations are valid ONLY when the acceleration (a) is constant and motion is along a straight line.

Variables:

  • u: Initial velocity
  • v: Final velocity
  • a: Constant acceleration
  • t: Time interval
  • s: Displacement

Motion Under Gravity

  • Special case where a = g (approx 9.8 m/s²)
  • Conventionally: a = +g (downward), a = -g (upward)

First Equation (v-t)

Relates final velocity, initial velocity, acceleration, and time.

v = u + at

Derived from definition of acceleration: a = (v-u)/t

Second Equation (s-t)

Relates displacement, initial velocity, time, and acceleration.

s = ut + ½ at²

Derived using average velocity: s = vavg × t = [(u+v)/2] × t and v = u+at

Third Equation (v-s)

Relates final velocity, initial velocity, acceleration, and displacement.

v² = u² + 2as

Derived by eliminating 't' from the first two equations.

Graphical Representation of Motion

Position-Time (x-t) Graph

  • Slope gives Instantaneous Velocity (v = dx/dt).
  • Straight line parallel to time axis: Object at rest (v=0).
  • Straight line with constant slope: Uniform velocity (v=constant, a=0).
  • Curve (Parabola for const. a): Non-uniform velocity.
  • Steeper slope means higher speed.
  • Slope can be positive, negative, or zero.

Velocity-Time (v-t) Graph

  • Slope gives Instantaneous Acceleration (a = dv/dt).
  • Area under the curve gives Displacement (s = ∫v dt).
  • Straight line parallel to time axis: Uniform velocity (a=0).
  • Straight line with constant slope: Uniform acceleration (a=constant).
  • Curve: Non-uniform acceleration.
  • Area above time axis is positive displacement, below is negative.

Acceleration-Time (a-t) Graph

  • Area under the curve gives Change in Velocity (Δv = ∫a dt).
  • Straight line parallel to time axis: Uniform acceleration.
  • Line coinciding with time axis (a=0): Uniform velocity.
  • Varying line/curve: Non-uniform acceleration.

Relative Velocity (1-D)

  • Velocity of A relative to B: VAB = VA - VB
  • Velocity of B relative to A: VBA = VB - VA
  • Note: VAB = -VBA
  • If moving in same direction, relative speed is |VA - VB|.
  • If moving in opposite directions, relative speed is |VA + VB|.
  • Important for problems involving overtakes, crossings, etc.
